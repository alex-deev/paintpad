About PaintPad

A browser-based drawing editor — a modern take on MS Paint. No account, no upload, no server: everything you draw stays in your own browser.

Open the editor.

What it does

  • Pencil, eraser, line, rectangle, ellipse, flood fill and rectangular selection
  • Move, delete, copy, cut and paste a selection
  • A 24-colour palette, the system colour picker, and hex entry
  • Real transparency, preserved through export
  • Autosave, so reloading brings your drawing back
  • PNG export at three sizes

How it works

The canvas is always derived from a command log. Drawing, replay and undo all run through the same code path, so a committed pixel and a replayed pixel are the same pixel. Autosave stores that log, which means your drawing comes back by replaying the same path live drawing took.

Two consequences are worth knowing. Backgrounds are composited beneath the artwork rather than baked into it, so erasing and then changing the background behaves the way you would expect. And fills store the parameters of a fill rather than the resulting pixels, which keeps your history small enough to save.

Export sizes

Export offers three sizes, all derived from the document itself rather than fixed pixel counts. The largest is the document's true resolution; the others are downscales of it. No option ever enlarges the image, because a larger PNG is not more detail.

Known limitations

  • No image import. Opening an existing PNG is not yet supported.
  • The clipboard is internal. Paste works inside PaintPad, but does not exchange images with other applications or the system clipboard yet.
  • Fills stop at a freehand edge. Freehand strokes are antialiased, so a fill meets the outline along a ramp of partially transparent pixels and an exact colour match stops just short of it.
  • One document at a time. There is a single saved document per browser, not a library of files.
